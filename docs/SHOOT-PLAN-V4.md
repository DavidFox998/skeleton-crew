# SHOOT PLAN — Story v4, LIVE (companion to SHOOTING-SCRIPT-V4.md)

## The shoot in one sentence

One room, one evening into night, one actor, six setups — the house is
dressed once and the desks are angles of the same room; everything the
house "does" (receipts, stamps, verdicts) is either a real prop printed
from the running software or post work by Talitha.

## Setups (shoot in this order, not story order)

1. **DOOR** — cold open + Beat 5 (shoot both while the light rig is up):
   coat, lantern lit (open) / unlit (close). Rain is post; wet the coat.
2. **REGISTER** — Beat 1 + Beat 4 voice moments play off-camera later;
   desk with ledger, wax, stamp, intake sheet.
3. **ESTATE** — Beat 2: the same desk redressed — two wills, the letter,
   receipt stack growing beside the desk.
4. **NOTICES** — Beat 3: a corner — galley proof, a composing stick or
   any press-like iron; smaller framing, flatter light.
5. **ARCHIVE** — Beat 4: drawer wall (any bank of drawers/boxes in
   shadow), candle, the receipt and pen close-ups; hands mostly.
6. **INSERTS** — letter set down; pen altering the receipt; stamp
   falling (post type, but shoot the hand motion); receipts printing —
   Talitha supplies REAL receipts from the running desks beforehand to
   print as props.

## Performance notes

- Desk replies are NOT played on set. Silas plays to counted pauses
  (marked in rehearsal with Talitha reading the desk lines over the
  phone once, then silent counts). Three takes per setup.
- Silas's arc in the body: Beat 1 amused → Beat 2 hopeful, then the
  stillness after "unmeasured" → Beat 3 too casual → Beat 4 careful
  hands → Beat 5 a man asking a door a question.

## Camera and light

- Phone, locked exposure/focus per setup, on a stack of books or any
  fixed support. No zooms; two framings per setup (wide + close).
- Candle + lantern + one warm practical. Nothing white, nothing bright.
- Record 60 seconds of room tone before wrapping (the house's silence
  is a track).

## Edit doctrine (David, 2026-11-03 22:30) — PACE

The film MOVES. The timing margin is found in the edit, not in script
cuts: scenes are cut into pieces and interwoven (two scenes as four
alternating pieces), camera movement carries energy, and every cut takes
its hunk — expect each scene to compress roughly 25% in the cutting.
Silences are staged full-length on set and compressed in the edit by
cross-cutting (receipt close-ups over desk reads; the replay beginning
under the archive hold). Do not pre-cut dialogue to fit the clock; fit
the clock in post.
