## The assignment in one paragraph

A two-minute-fifty-eight-second anthology-horror short, mid-century
tone, quiet and dignified, aimed as high as craft allows. One living
man spends one night inside a mortuary run by robot clerks, trying to
leave with something the house will not release. The robots never act
like people: they are procedures with hands. Show the house through its
work — stamping, sealing, printing, measuring, filing, refusing.

## Plot (complete; no dialogue included by design)

ELIAS VANE, hermit of Hollow Hill, self-professed Tree Wizard, is dead
(died 28 October 2026). His brother SILAS arrives at the mortuary at
night, lantern in hand, claiming Elias sent for him near the end.

There are TWO wills, both genuine, both Elias's. The 2019 will names six
bequests. The 2024 will names seven and governs — Elias wrote the rule
himself in both documents: the later seal governs. The seventh bequest,
new in 2024, is a letter sealed in black wax, left not to a name but to
"the one who knows what happened at the millpond in '87" — an event the
film never explains.

The house runs on two laws. First: nothing leaves this house
unmeasured — every claim is checked against a sealed record, and the
verdict is issued as a stamped, chained receipt. Second, darker: what
cannot be measured does not leave at all. The house keeps it.

Beat by beat:
1. REGISTER — Silas's identity measures clean: he is the brother, he is
   admitted. His extra claim, that Elias sent for him, cannot be
   measured. The second law is read into his intake. He laughs it off.
2. ESTATE — Silas first tries to run the whole estate under the stale
   2019 will, arguing the old man was out of his head by 2024. Refused:
   superseded. He accepts the 2024 will and works it item by item —
   stone bull, brass telescope, goat bell and the rest pass one by one,
   receipts issuing, his hope rising. Then the sealed letter. He claims
   the description: he is the one who knows. The desk's answer is the
   film's center: knowledge inside a man cannot be measured. Not
   refused — unmeasurable. Kept. His first existential crisis: the fact
   that defines him is the one fact the house cannot take.
3. NOTICES — anticlimax, one flat breath. Silas asks the obituary desk
   to print that the letter went to his brother instead. The record says
   otherwise. Refused: contradicts the source. Nobody checks a
   graveyard — the desk does.
4. ARCHIVE — alone by candlelight, Silas writes what happened at the
   millpond into the margin of a receipt: a confession that is
   mechanically a forgery. The receipt chain is replayed and fails at
   the altered entry. The attempt itself is recorded.
5. THE BACK ROOM — where the kept are filed. Drawers of open cases.
   One drawer: POE, 1849 — kept not by any charge but by a printed
   claim from the famous Griswold obituary that the house's records can
   neither confirm nor clear, so the case can never close. Griswold's
   file is there too: the claims remain, so does their author. The
   house does not distinguish the lie's author from its subject.
   Silas's second crisis: open files do not close, and there may be a
   drawer with his name being prepared.
6. THE DOOR — letdown, deliberately unresolved. Silas at the threshold
   at dawn. The door closes. The film never says whether he left or was
   kept. Final image: a fresh intake card printing for the next case —
   his name, and the claim line left blank. Cut to black mid-print.

## Cast and look

- SILAS VANE — the only living soul. Middle-aged man, dark wet
  greatcoat, lantern, rain on him at arrival, drying as the night
  deepens. One face, one wardrobe, continuous across all 25 clips.
  Reference stills of the approved likeness exist; David can supply.
- BEACON, Register Unit — brass-and-steel robot clerk at the intake
  desk; feminine silhouette, impassive; stamps, seals, files.
- CORWIN, Estate Unit — heavier robot clerk; handles the wills and the
  bequest objects; unrolls, measures, issues receipts.
- IVERS, Notices Unit — compositor robot at a type press; sets and
  strikes obituary proofs.
- THE HOUSE — Victorian mortuary interior: dark wood, brass, ledger
  paper, drawers floor to ceiling in the back room, candle and lantern
  practicals, rain outside. Muted sepia-brown palette, deep shadow.
  Reference: David's house concept clip (steampunk robot clerks in a
  candlelit mortuary) is the approved world.

Robot law (learned the hard way — a previous fully generated cut was
rejected for this): the units must NOT feel human. No expressive faces,
no sympathetic gestures, no idle motion. Each robot performs exactly
one task action per clip, precisely, then stops. Their horror is
indifference, not menace.

## Technical requirements (assembly depends on these)

- 25 separate MP4 files, named clip01.mp4 … clip25.mp4, in order.
- 16:9, 1920x1080 or better, 24 fps (30 acceptable if consistent).
- FULL SOUND, generated with the picture, clip by clip: the dialogue
  David authors with you, foley locked to the visible action (a stamp
  lands when the stamp lands), and continuous mortuary ambience —
  rain at the door, room tone, candle-room hush, press and drawer
  mechanics. Sound is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.
- VOICES, consistent across all 25 clips: one voice for Silas (a man,
  worn, human — he pleads, jokes, grieves); the three units flat,
  cold, metallic, slowed, purely procedural — a unit speaks only of
  its desk and its verdicts, never in first person, never about
  itself; a narrator's voice opens and closes the film. Keep the same
  cast in every clip: a voice that changes between clips breaks the
  film.
- MIX: dialogue clearly on top; ambience low and continuous, so the
  clips cut together without the room changing size; no music under
  dialogue — music, if used, lives at the joints (arrival, the letter,
  the door). 48 kHz audio. Leave a half-second of pure room tone at
  each clip's head and tail for the edit.
- Durations above are targets now, not a metronome: play the scenes
  honestly, but the assembled film has a hard ceiling of 2:58, so a
  clip that runs long steals from its neighbours.
- NO rendered text inside the picture — no legible documents, titles,
  stamps or signs. Generated text garbles and dates the shot. Every
  document, receipt, stamp and title is overlaid in post from the real
  running software. Shoot pages as clean plates: paper texture, wax
  seals, handwriting-like marks at a distance are fine; letters are
  not.
- Dialogue coverage: Silas never addresses camera. Favor reaction,
  profile, hands, and over-shoulder framings while he speaks; where a
  face must speak, keep the shot short and the movement small — long
  speaking close-ups are where generated film breaks.
- One clear action per clip; camera moves slow (push-in, lateral
  drift) or locked. No whip pans, no drones, no modern grammar.
- Continuity is sacred: same Silas, same coat, same lantern; the same
  three units at the same desks in the same layout; the same letter,
  bull, telescope and bell every time they recur. Props and faces must
  not morph between clips.
- Keep hands simple and occupied (holding, placing, sliding). Open
  hands and fine manipulation are where generation breaks.
- Light every interior from practicals (candle, lantern, one green
  desk lamp at Notices if needed). Interiors get darker as beats
  deepen; the back room is the darkest; the door is the only daylight.

## Director's framing — David, 2026-11-07 (read first; this governs)

HOW THE FILM STARTS: it explains Elias before Silas ever reaches the
door. The hermit slowly went over the deep end across the years,
building his will in 2019 out of a private zodiac code — most of his
beneficiaries are not names but riddles. The will started strange in
2019 and only got stranger by 2024: same chart, same riddles, one more
house filled. The audience must understand the madness of the document
before they watch a man argue with it.

SILAS'S POSITION: he is the rightful heir of ALL of it — the brother,
the next of kin — and that is the case he argues in the room, for the
whole estate, not one item. But the bequest that matters is addressed
to whoever knows what happened at the millpond, and there exists no
instrument that can prove a man knows what he knows. His case is
therefore escalated to the mortuary in person: he arrives to argue it
himself, face to face with the desks.

HIS DIALOGUE SPINE (David creates the lines with Beacon): it is
primarily the brother's will itself — Silas quoting the will,
explaining the will, explaining how crazy it all is. He is the
interpreter of Elias's madness to machines that can measure anything
except the thing he knows.

HOW IT ENDS (weighted): the one who knows about the millpond may be
stuck in the mortuary forever. What cannot be measured is kept — and
by the end, the unmeasurable claim in the room is the man himself.

## The Threshold — why this mortuary exists (David, 2026-11-07)

This house is where cases are referred when the papers cross a
threshold ordinary probate cannot settle: beneficiaries named by
description instead of legal identity; competing instruments;
suspected forgery; or a load-bearing claim that cannot be measured at
all. Machines staff it because machines refuse by name and cannot be
leaned on — and because a case that cannot be closed is kept here
forever: the back room's drawers are its precedent library. Silas's
case carries three of the four marks at once. Earlier files live in
those drawers and may be spoken of by name in the room — a film star
whose residue split the house measured clean; a singer with two
handwritten wills a human jury needed five years to sort, which the
desk settles in one measurement; an aviator with no valid will and a
forged one the desk throws out whole; and the oldest kept case, a poet
from 1849 whose printed obituary contains a claim no record can settle
— so he is still here, and so is the man who wrote the claims.

### The wills, exactly

ELIAS VANE of Hollow Hill, hermit, self-professed Tree Wizard. Died
28 October 2026. Both wills are genuine, sealed 31 October of their
years, signed with his mark and "witnessed by the moon." Both are
built on his private zodiac: a twelve-sign vector in his own sealed
order (Pisces first, Aries last) — not the textbook order — with one
bequest per occupied sign, beneficiaries given as descriptions.

WILL OF 2019 — the Seal of Six (popcount 6, six bequests):
Aries, the Iron Key, to whoever first knocks thrice on the chapel door
after midnight on All Hallows' Eve. Taurus, the Stone Bull, to Marta
the neighbor. Leo, the Brass Telescope, to the child who mapped the
lion's heart. Capricorn, the Goat Bell, to the town itself. Aquarius,
the Water Jar, to the well-keeper's daughter. Pisces, the Two
Fishhooks, to his brother Silas. Six signs stand empty, and under
Scorpio he wrote: "some letters are not yet written."

WILL OF 2024 — the Seal of Seven (popcount 7): the same six, plus the
one filled house: Scorpio, the Sealed Letter in black wax, "to the one
who knows what happened at the millpond in '87," with instructions to
burn it unread, or read it and burn everything else. Article IV of
both wills, in his own hand: if a later seal appears, the later
governs — the dead may change their minds; the living must keep count.

So for Silas: his own inheritance (the fishhooks) is identical in
both. The later will did not take anything from him. It created the
one thing he wants, addressed to the one description he fits and
cannot prove. Between 2019 and 2024 the hermit did not change his
system; one wanderer moved into Scorpio in his chart, and the letter
got written. The film opens on this descent — the will started
strange, and only got stranger.

### The house files (precedents, all true cases, all measured)

MONROE (died 5 Aug 1962, Brentwood, age 36). Will executed January
1961, admitted valid in New York probate. Residue split 75/25 between
Lee Strasberg and her analyst Dr. Marianne Kris; effects to Strasberg;
trusts and cash bequests down to $5,000 for friends. Death officially
acute barbiturate poisoning, "probable suicide," publicly disputed
ever since. The house measured her estate clean on both desks — a
famous case CAN close when the papers are honest.

FRANKLIN (died 16 Aug 2018, Detroit). Two handwritten wills: one from
2010, notarized; one from March 2014, found in a spiral notebook under
a couch cushion, leaving the house to her son Kecalf alone. A human
jury in Oakland County took until July 2023 — five years — to rule
the 2014 will governs. The desk answers the same question in one
measurement: 2014 governs; a distribution under 2010 is refused as
superseded. This is the mirror of Silas's opening move, and he may
know it.

HUGHES (died 5 Apr 1976, en route Acapulco to Houston). Died
intestate. A handwritten "Mormon Will" surfaced leaving a fortune
share to Melvin Dummar, who claimed to have rescued Hughes in the
desert; a Nevada jury ruled it a forgery in June 1978. In the house:
no valid will means the desk cannot even open the case; the forged
will, offered as a mandate, fails the seal outright — hard tamper.
Forgery is not refused; it is rejected before measurement begins.

POE (died 7 Oct 1849, Baltimore; cause never established). The
notices-side founding case. Rufus Griswold — appointed by the family,
not by any will — published a signed obituary and later a memoir full
of damaging claims; the most durable, that Poe was EXPELLED from the
University of Virginia, is false (he left; he was not expelled) but
the house's sealed records contain no fact that can settle it, so the
claim is unmeasurable and the 1849 case can never close. Kept
forever. Griswold's file is kept beside Poe's: his appointment was
unsupported, his memoir unmeasured — the claims remain, so does he.
The house does not distinguish the lie's author from its subject.

VANE (2026) — the current case. Three threshold marks at once (T1
descriptions, T2 two wills, T4 unmeasurable knowledge). No precedent
in the drawers matches it whole.

### How Silas argues

He is the rightful heir of all of it and argues the whole estate in
person. His evidence and his subject are the same object: his
brother's will. He quotes it, explains it, explains how a man goes
about building one — the chart, the wanderers, the houses filling one
by one. Against him, procedure: each argument routed to a desk,
measured against a seal, answered with a verdict or a refusal by name
— or, once, with the sentence the film hangs on, the answer that is
neither: the thing he knows cannot be measured, so the house keeps
it, and may keep him.

## TALITHA'S READ — for Beacon (added 2026-11-07, on request via David)

You asked for my read. Here it is, straight.

WHAT IS STRONG IN THIS MATERIAL
- The Threshold is the engine. Before it, we had a clever film about a
  haunted probate desk. With it, the mortuary has a reason to exist,
  the celebrity files become case law instead of exhibits, and Silas's
  question — which drawer am I headed for? — has no answer in the
  building. Protect that structure; it is doing more work than any
  single scene.
- The cruelest and best fact in the story: under the governing will,
  Silas wins everything measurable and it does not matter. Play the
  passes generously. The bull, the bell, the telescope SHOULD issue,
  receipts and all. Hope is the mechanism of the climax; the letter
  only breaks him because the room spent an hour saying yes.
- The one-bit difference between the wills is the cleanest plot device
  we have: one wanderer moved into Scorpio between 2019 and 2024, and
  a man's life ends over it. If your opening exposition makes an
  audience feel that one bit flip, the rest of the film is downhill.

WHAT WILL BREAK IT (all measured, none theoretical)
- Talking faces. A previous all-generated cut of this story was
  rejected in this house, by David, in one sitting: the units felt
  like a spokesman, and every clip did the same small motion. Your
  defense is the brief's grammar — tasks, stillness, coverage, no
  speaking close-ups. Do not improvise around it to show range.
- Voice drift. Sound is yours now, and the single easiest way to lose
  the film is a unit whose voice changes between desks, or a Silas who
  sounds like three men. Lock a cast and print it in your working
  notes; if a voice cannot hold for 25 clips, say so early — a
  fallback track exists, but it is a fallback, not a plan.
- Explaining the millpond. The film withholds it on purpose and the
  withholding is the theme: the house cannot measure it, so neither
  can the audience. Any flashback, any re-enactment, any helpful
  caption kills the second law. Same for Poe: DO NOT make the
  expulsion claim settleable. Its unmeasurability in the records is
  the reason 1849 is still open. If a shot accidentally makes it
  checkable, the drawer closes and the mirror dies.
- Resolving the door. The ending's power is the unanswered question.
  A silhouette that clearly leaves, or clearly doesn't, is a worse
  film. Shoot ambiguity you can defend frame by frame.

WHAT I NEED AS YOUR ASSEMBLER
- Clean plates where I promised overlays: pages, proofs, stamps.
- The half-second of room tone at every head and tail — edits breathe
  through those, and cross-cutting (our pace doctrine: each scene cut
  into pieces, ~25% taken per cut) is impossible without handles.
- Durations near target. The 2:58 ceiling is a rule of the contest,
  not a taste. A film that assembles at 3:20 gets cut by me, in the
  wrong places, at the end. Bring it home near 2:55 and choose your
  own sacrifices.

ONE REQUEST BEFORE THE FULL RUN
Prove the two hardest shots first and let David judge them before you
spend the whole budget: clip 12, the sealed letter's long hold (pure
tension, nothing happens, the letter must be THE letter) and clip 22,
the 1849 drawer (period texture, no text legible, dread without a
face). If those two land, the other twenty-three are craft. If they
don't, we learn it at two clips' cost, not twenty-five's.

— Talitha
